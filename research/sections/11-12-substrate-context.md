## 11. Substrate: what an open-source studio would be built on (B)

The preceding parts of the stage view describe what a composer-centred studio should *do*; this section surveys what it would be *made of*: notation engines and editors (B1), symbolic toolkits (B2), the runtime carrying MIDI, audio and plugin integration (B3), the corpora any learned component must be trained on — whose licences, not sizes, govern what an open project may ship (B4) — and the pedagogy of music-as-computation (B5). Versions and dates are reported where the underlying notes verified them and hedged where they did not.

### 11.1 B1 Notation engines and editors

**Browser engraving.** Verovio is the strongest open route to an engraving-quality, *annotatable* score in a browser: a C++20 library (dual LGPL-3.0/GPL-3.0, v6.2.1 May 2026) compiled to WebAssembly, reading MEI natively plus MusicXML, Humdrum and ABC and emitting SVG, MIDI and a *timemap* for score–audio synchronisation [@pugin2014verovio]. Two properties matter for annotation: every rendered glyph carries an element id mapping back to the source encoding, making click-to-annotate and scribble overlays anchored to *symbolic* elements feasible rather than merely graphical; and MEI encodes editorial layers, so the difference between what the human wrote and what a machine proposed can live in the score file [@pugin2014verovio]. mei-friend demonstrates the pattern: MEI code beside a live Verovio rendering, GitHub-backed commits, and W3C Web Annotations on element ids [@goebl2023meifriend]. Interchange must nonetheless be MusicXML 4.0, implemented by over 250 applications; its JSON successor MNX remained unfinished through August 2026, and neither has an annotation model beyond directive text (S2.1) [@w3c2021musicxml40].

**The alternatives trade annotation depth for simplicity.** OpenSheetMusicDisplay (BSD-3, v2.0.0 June 2026) lays out MusicXML automatically but is explicitly a renderer, not an editor [@osmd2026], sitting on VexFlow (MIT), a low-level engraver still the right building block for chord grids and sketch overlays [@vexflow2024]. alphaTab (MPL-2.0) is one of the few open engines shipping notation and synchronised SoundFont playback together [@alphatab2026], and abcjs re-renders ABC to SVG on every keystroke with playback — the cheapest text-to-notation round trip, and important because ABC is what specialised music LLMs emit most fluently (S3.6) [@abcjs2026; @walshaw2011abc]. LilyPond is the batch, text-compiled engraving benchmark and literal ancestor of the compile metaphor [@lilypond2026; @nienhuys2003lilypond], while Humdrum contributes an encoding idea rather than an engine: parallel spines put a `**harm` column beside the notes in one plain-text file [@huron1995humdrum].

**MuseScore is the editor to integrate with, not to compete against.** MuseScore Studio 4.x is a GPL-3 C++/Qt 6.9 editor with SMuFL fonts, VST3 hosting, MusicXML and MIDI interchange, MuseSounds libraries behind the proprietary Muse Hub and a QML plugin API extended in 4.6 [@musescore2026studio; @musegroup2025musescore46]. Two facts shape integration: the "extensions framework" meant to replace that read-mostly API was deferred, leaving external scripted manipulation a pain point; and 4.7 (2026) shipped a beta MP3-to-MSCZ converter, Muse Group's only shipping AI feature, its announced Cantai choir integration delayed [@musescore2026studio; @musegroup2025musescore46]. MuseScore Studio 5, begun December 2025, targets DAW-like automation, a piano-roll, audio staves and MIDI mapping: the notation editor is converging on the DAW from the opposite direction to Suno Studio [@musescore2026studio; @suno2026]. The most-used free notation editor is thus open source, speaks the interchange formats, feeds licence-clean corpora and is entering the studio's territory with no generative capability — an installed base for a studio that treats MSCZ and MusicXML as first-class.

**The commercial landscape and the web platforms.** MakeMusic ended development and sales of Finale on 26 August 2024 and steered users to Dorico Pro at a $149 crossgrade [@makemusic2024finale]; Dorico 6 and 6.2 added proofreading and engraving refinements but no machine learning [@steinberg2025dorico6], and Sibelius through February 2026 added themes, engraving rules and scripting, again with none [@avid2026sibelius]. On the web, Flat offers a collaborative editor with REST and embed APIs and per-edit version history, Noteflight a comparable but less open one [@flat2026; @flat2026history]; Soundslice aligns notation to reference recordings through explicit *syncpoints* behind a Data API, the machinery an "insert example audio" annotation needs [@soundslice2026]; and Hookpad's lead-sheet editor, with colour-coded Roman numerals and theory hints, hosts the Aria co-writer (C1, E3) [@donahue2024hookpadaria].

### 11.2 B2 Symbolic toolkits

**music21 is the reference analysis layer.** Cuthbert and Ariza's toolkit parses MusicXML, MIDI, ABC, Humdrum `**kern` and MEI into a hierarchical `Stream` model, writes MusicXML, MIDI and LilyPond, supplies key finding, `RomanNumeral` analysis, chord identification and voice-leading, and bundles a corpus including all 371 Riemenschneider Bach chorales, Palestrina and the Viennese quartets; v10.5.0 (June 2026) is BSD-3-Clause on Python 3.11 or later [@cuthbert2010music21]. Two limits bound its role: pure-Python parsing is too slow for corpus-scale preprocessing, and MusicXML round-tripping preserves pitch, rhythm and spanners but not layout, with MEI export absent [@cuthbert2010music21; @liao2024symusic]. It is an analysis backend and vocabulary source, not a real-time engine or a lossless notation store.

**The processing stack has consolidated.** symusic is an MIT C++20 core with nanobind bindings that loads and saves MIDI and ABC and applies vectorised transforms, reporting parsing hundreds of times faster than mido, pretty_midi and miditoolkit, and now serving as MidiTok's I/O backend [@liao2024symusic]. MidiTok (MIT) implements ten tokenisations — REMI, REMI+, MIDI-Like, TSD, Structured, CPWord, Octuple, MuMIDI, MMM and PerTok — with BPE and Unigram training on the Hugging Face backend; its MMM-style per-track-bar scheme maps onto region-scoped editing, making tokeniser choice an interface decision as much as a modelling one (S3.1, S3.2) [@fradet2021miditok; @ens2020mmm]. partitura (Apache-2.0, v1.9.0 May 2026) covers the axis the others do not, parsing MusicXML, MEI, `**kern` and MIDI into a `Score` with note arrays and computing score-to-performance alignment — the primitive connecting a passage played on a keyboard to the notation it annotates [@cancinochacon2022partitura; @karystinaios2023chordgnn]. MusPy, unmaintained since 2022, is now a design reference for dataset management, representations and objective metrics [@dong2020muspy].

**Legacy interoperability and adjacent toolkits.** The older stack — mido, pretty_midi, miditoolkit and note-seq with its `NoteSequence` protobuf, a proven design for an internal representation — cannot be discarded because public checkpoints assume it; these details are recorded as partially verified [@raffel2014prettymidi; @huang2020popmusictransformer; @hsiao2021compoundword]. Magenta.js showed small symbolic models run client-side, shipping TensorFlow.js ports of MusicVAE, Coconet and Piano Genie that powered the Bach Doodle and Cococo, but it is effectively frozen, so new browser deployments should plan on ONNX Runtime Web or WebGPU [@roberts2018magentajs; @huang2019bachdoodle; @louie2020cococo; @lyria2025livemusic]. Muskits-ESPnet is the open path from a symbolic line plus lyrics to sung audio [@wu2024muskitsespnet].

### 11.3 B3 Runtime and integration

**Web MIDI is the binding platform constraint.** In 2026 the Web MIDI API works in Chrome 43+, Edge 79+, Opera and Firefox 108+ behind a site-permission add-on, with global coverage around 81 per cent, but Safari on desktop and iOS supports it not at all and has published no roadmap, Apple citing fingerprinting [@w3c2026webmidi]. For a studio whose compose path includes playing material in from a keyboard this is decisive: a purely web deployment excludes Apple users from live input, making a desktop shell an architectural requirement. Web Audio, by contrast, is universal, AudioWorklet permits sample-accurate WebAssembly DSP off the main thread, and Tone.js (MIT) supplies the transport layer.

**Synthesis is ready in the browser and headless.** spessasynth is a pure TypeScript SF2/SF3/DLS synthesizer running in an AudioWorklet that also plays MIDI and renders offline to WAV, under Apache-2.0; FluidSynth (LGPL-2.1) remains the reference SoundFont synthesizer for offline rendering in research pipelines and SFZ supplies a redistributable sample body, these last two partial [@spessasynth2026; @liao2024symusic; @dong2020muspy]. A compiled score can thus be auditioned in the client and rendered headlessly for evaluation (E4) [@alphatab2026].

**Plugin protocols and integration precedents.** Composers work in DAWs, so a compiler that cannot appear inside one is an island. Only one protocol was verified directly: CLAP, announced June 2022 by u-he and Bitwig, an MIT-licensed C ABI with per-note modulation, supported by Bitwig, REAPER and FL Studio; VST3, Audio Units, ReaScript, Ableton Link's tempo synchronisation over a LAN and Max for Live are recorded as recalled rather than re-verified [@clap2022]. The deployment precedents matter more. Magenta Studio's five Max for Live devices operate on the selected clip under Apache-2.0, establishing "the AI operates on the clip you selected, inside your DAW" as the reference architecture [@roberts2019magentastudio]; MMM4Live carried the Metacreation lineage to the same surface but stayed a closed beta without a published study [@metacreation2023mmm4live; @ens2020mmm]; and Composer's Assistant in REAPER and MIDI-GPT in Cubase and Ableton are the fullest expressions of the pattern (S3.2) [@malandro2024composersassistant2; @pasquier2025midigpt]. AbletonMCP, an MIT-licensed Model Context Protocol server plus Remote Script, adds conversational control over tracks, clips, notes and arrangements [@ahuja2025abletonmcp]. The founder's own instrument sits at the easy end: the Nord Stage family reportedly transmits on a global MIDI channel with program change and class-compliant USB-MIDI, making it an ordinary Web MIDI device in a Chromium host — though this entry is unverified recall [@clavia2023nordstage4].

### 11.4 B4 Datasets and corpora, and what their licences permit

Generation papers routinely report dataset sizes and omit licences; for an open project the licence is the more consequential number, because it decides whether a trained artefact can be released at all.

| Licence class | Corpora (size; licence) | What a released artefact may do |
|---|---|---|
| Permissive (CC0 / CC-BY) | PDMX, >250k public-domain MusicXML (CC-BY 4.0) [@long2024pdmx]; OpenScore Lieder, 1,200+ proofread songs (CC0), and String Quartets [@gotham2022openscore]; GiantMIDI-Piano, 10,855 pieces, ~1,237 h [@kong2020giantmidi]; ATEPP, 11,674 performances, ~1,000 h [@zhang2022atepp]; Bach Doodle, 21.6M melodies [@huang2019bachdoodle]; Lakh MIDI, roughly 176,600 files (reportedly CC-BY *on the collection*) [@raffel2016lakh] | Release weights; subject to the Lakh caveat |
| Non-commercial (CC-BY-NC-SA) | MAESTRO, ~200 h / 1,276 performances (partial) [@hawthorne2019maestro]; Aria-MIDI, 1.19M files / ~100,629 h transcribed piano [@bradshaw2025ariamidi]; Hooktheory/TheoryTab via Sheet Sage, ~50 h of functional-harmony analyses aligned to audio [@donahue2022sheetsage]; DCML corpora (partial) [@hentschel2021mozart]; MidiCaps (partial) [@melechovsky2024midicaps] | Published experiments and prototypes only |
| Restricted or murky | MetaMIDI, 436,631 files (registration, no redistribution) [@ens2021metamidi]; Los Angeles MIDI, ~405k files (provenance unstated) [@lev2024lamidi]; MetaScore, 963k MuseScore-sourced scores [@xu2025metascore]; Wikifonia (site closed 2013) [@wang2020pop909]; musescore.com scrapes [@xmader2021musescore] | Nothing shippable; research-only at best |

The Lakh caveat is the most widely ignored fact in the symbolic literature: the CC-BY licence covers the *collection*, while the files are user transcriptions of copyrighted songs, so commercial use is a grey zone and quality is uneven [@raffel2016lakh]. PDMX is the clean alternative — public-domain-only MusicXML from musescore.com with ratings enabling quality filtering, released CC-BY and shown to improve multitrack generation when rating-filtered, which its authors call the largest available copyright-free symbolic dataset [@long2024pdmx]; for a notation-first studio it is doubly attractive because it is score-level, carrying dynamics, articulations and lyrics.

**Analysis corpora.** For machine-proposed harmonic annotations (S2.2), two corpora supply both training data and a format. When in Rome holds roughly 2,000 Roman-numeral analyses of about 1,500 works in RomanText (`.rntxt`, parsed by music21), the largest open training set for the task and the basis of AugmentedNet and RNBert [@gotham2019wheninrome; @cuthbert2010music21; @napoleslopez2021augmentednet; @sailor2024rnbert]. The DCML corpora instead store expert chord, phrase and cadence labels *inside* MuseScore files as staff text [@hentschel2021mozart; @neuwirth2018abc] — annotations living in the score file being exactly the pattern the studio needs.

**Captions, chords and audio.** MidiCaps pairs over 168,000 Lakh-derived files with LLM-generated captions from extracted tempo, chord, instrument and mood features [@melechovsky2024midicaps]; MusicCaps and Song Describer are the audio-side caption sets used by nearly every text-to-music model [@agostinelli2023musiclm; @manco2023songdescriber]; and MetaScore's 963,000 MuseScore-sourced scores were converted to REMI+/MMT tokens rather than generated as notation [@xu2025metascore]. Chordonomicon adds over 666,000 chord progressions with section, genre and harmonic-function labels, its licence partial [@kantarelis2024chordonomicon]. Slakh2100's 2,100 Lakh files rendered into 145 hours of stems give exact symbolic-to-stem pairs for rendering evaluation, alongside MusicNet, MedleyDB and MUSDB18, all partially verified [@manilow2019slakh]. Among the small classics POP909's melody-versus-accompaniment split is a direct analogue of compiling a piano part from a lead sheet [@wang2020pop909; @huang2017coconet], and HumTrans's 56.22 hours of humming, whose baselines reach roughly 6.8 per cent note F1, quantify why an interactive correction step is mandatory (S1.2) [@liu2023humtrans; @gupta2024dynhumtrans; @cartwright2015vocalsketch].

The shipping rule follows. Anything released with weights should be trained on the permissive tier — PDMX and OpenScore for notation, GiantMIDI-Piano and ATEPP for expressive piano, the composer's own material for personal style — with per-file licence metadata retained; the non-commercial tier is legitimate for published experiments provided the boundary is documented; the restricted tier is for reading about, not training on. Composer's Assistant, trained only on permissively licensed MIDI, shows a competitive infilling model can be built inside these constraints [@malandro2024composersassistant2].

### 11.5 B5 Education and the pedagogy of music-as-computation

**Two MIT courses bracket the design space.** Cuthbert's 21M.383 Computational Music Theory and Analysis teaches symbolic computational musicology entirely through Python and music21 notebooks, from score representation and `Stream` hierarchies through MusicXML to corpus statistics on the Bach chorales, Roman numerals and algorithmic composition; one session treats the 371 chorales as a queryable dataset — the corpus that trains Coconet — and the materials are CC-BY-NC-SA on OpenCourseWare [@cuthbert2023ocw21m383; @cuthbert2010music21; @huang2017coconet]. It is a tested pedagogy for treating notation as data and analysis as a program. Egozy's 21M.385 Interactive Music Systems is its interactional complement, with students building real-time Python and Kivy applications using FluidSynth-backed synthesis, MIDI sequencing, generative composition, gesture sensors and game-style graphics [@egozy21m385]; its notation-free architecture is the deliberate inverse of the studio's stance. Egozy's writing on the Harmonix titles analyses how they designed for *musical expression* within game constraints, and his in-browser score following is a component for aligning live playing to notation [@egozy2016harmonix; @egozy2018concertcue].

**Music-as-code platforms show the metaphor scales to novices.** TunePad has learners write Python in notebook-like cells producing beats, basslines, chords and melodies with real-time playback, with Playbooks as combined tutorial-and-code documents; its cell interface is a direct precedent for a composition-as-program editor [@horn2022tunepad]. EarSketch has students place licensed loops on tracks and impose algorithmic structure through `fitMedia` and `makeBeat`, with reported gains in intent to persist in computing especially among under-represented students, its claimed million-plus reach recorded as partial [@freeman2019earsketch]. Sonic Pi puts `live_loop` at the centre — code edited while it sounds — an *edit-while-playing* loop worth borrowing for annotate-compile cycles [@aaron2016sonicpi], and Hookpad's colour-coded Roman numerals and chord hints are a pedagogy embedded in a composition tool [@donahue2024hookpadaria].

**Practice apps and games.** The commercial tutor ecosystem — Yousician, Simply Piano, Flowkey, Melodics, Piano Marvel, SmartMusic, Chordify and Moises — rests on real-time pitch and onset detection, gamified progression and, increasingly, source separation and chord recognition that turn any recording into practice material; efficacy studies are few and the entry is partial, but these products set expectations for immediate feedback on playing and singing, and Moises shows stem separation as a route for ingesting example audio [@yousician2026; @moises2025aistudio]. Miller's ethnography of Guitar Hero and Rock Band argues that such games constitute musical performance and informal learning [@miller2009schizophonic], and Harmonix's DropMix and Fuser made *arrangement by stem selection* a game mechanic with the engine handling key and tempo matching [@harmonix2020fuser]. One caution qualifies the picture: the first empirical studies of generative AI in composition, verified only through Crossref metadata and of mixed venue quality, include an experiment on AI ideation in composition tasks — not in a classroom setting — reporting reduced originality of output [@oros2026helpthathurts], very possibly the same study as the CMU poster discussed under E1 rather than a second result [@oros2026cmu] — indicative rather than conclusive, but supporting the view that assistance at ideation is the risky configuration and assistance at realisation the safer one.

### 11.6 Implications for the studio

The build-versus-adopt line can be drawn sharply. The studio should adopt Verovio and MEI as its score surface and annotation store, MusicXML 4.0 as its interchange boundary, music21 as its analysis backend, symusic and MidiTok as its corpus and tokenisation layer, partitura for score–performance alignment, and spessasynth or FluidSynth for audition and headless rendering [@pugin2014verovio; @w3c2021musicxml40; @cuthbert2010music21; @liao2024symusic; @fradet2021miditok; @cancinochacon2022partitura; @spessasynth2026]. It should not build an engraver, a parser, a tokeniser or a synthesizer. It should build the three things the substrate lacks: a typed, time- and element-anchored annotation layer that survives round trips through MusicXML and MEI; a lossless bridge between an LLM-friendly textual representation — ABC being the practical candidate, with Humdrum's spines as the model for carrying marks alongside notes — and engraving-grade notation; and the compile orchestration routing each annotation to the right specialised model [@walshaw2011abc; @abcjs2026; @huron1995humdrum]. The integration target is MuseScore rather than a fork, with a CLAP or VST3 bridge and a desktop shell to escape the Web MIDI gap [@musescore2026studio; @clap2022; @w3c2026webmidi].

The data decision is a licensing decision and should be made once, at the start: training only on the permissive tier costs scale relative to Aria-MIDI's hundred thousand hours but buys the right to release weights [@long2024pdmx; @gotham2022openscore; @kong2020giantmidi; @zhang2022atepp; @bradshaw2025ariamidi; @malandro2024composersassistant2]. Per-file licence metadata carried through preprocessing lets any released artefact state its provenance, which C2 and C3 show is becoming a regulatory as well as an ethical requirement.

Three substrate gaps are research opportunities rather than engineering chores. No notation format has an adequate annotation model: MEI's editorial layers come closest and Humdrum spines and RomanText cover parallel data and harmony, while MusicXML and MNX cover neither [@pugin2014verovio; @gotham2019wheninrome; @huron1995humdrum; @w3c2021musicxml40]. Score corpora are large but *annotated* corpora are small — When in Rome and the DCML sub-corpora are effectively the whole supply of expert harmonic annotation — which caps what an annotate step can learn and argues for treating human corrections as training data from day one [@gotham2019wheninrome; @hentschel2021mozart]. And the education literature validates the compile metaphor while compiling loops and samples rather than *notation*; a notation-native compiler is the missing member of that family [@horn2022tunepad; @freeman2019earsketch; @aaron2016sonicpi].

## 12. Context: products, law, ethics and economics (C)

The environment around a composer-centred studio changed more between 2024 and 2026 than the research did. This section covers the product landscape (C1), bifurcated into audio text-to-song platforms pulled into label-licensed walled gardens and symbolic assistants embedded in existing tools while the notation incumbents ship nothing generative; law and policy as of September 2026 (C2), where German courts have held memorisation in model weights to be an infringing reproduction while United States doctrine still requires human authorship; the provenance and disclosure infrastructure that emerged in response (C3); and the economics and creator attitudes that define the studio's audience (C4).

### 12.1 C1 The product landscape 2024–2026

**The audio family and its drift toward editing.** Suno exemplifies the paradigm and the pressure toward editability within it: around the generator it built Suno Studio, announced 24 September 2025 as a browser "generative audio workstation" with a multitrack timeline, stem separation and per-section regeneration, with Studio 2.0 on 13 August 2026 adding MIDI import, recording and editing plus a chat-bar assistant — the MIDI living inside the workstation, not in the generator's conditioning path [@suno2026]. Effective 3 September 2026 as part of the Warner settlement, downloads were capped at seven lifetime on the free tier, twenty per month on Pro and sixty on Premier, with prior models to be retired when licensed ones launch [@suno2026; @wmg2025suno]. Udio traced the same arc earlier, then disabled downloads in October 2025 and announced a 2026 platform in which creations stay inside the walled garden, fingerprinted and filtered [@udio2025; @umg2025udio]. Google's Lyria family offers the most complete audio editing loop among large-vendor products, with the Music AI Sandbox's Create, Extend and Edit verbs, Lyria RealTime's prompt-weighted streaming, SynthID watermarking, Lyria 3 in the Gemini app from February 2026 and the acquisition of Producer.ai — formerly Riffusion — announced on 24 February 2026 and rebranded by the trade press as Google Flow Music that April [@google2025lyria2; @lyria2025livemusic; @forsgren2022riffusion]. Eleven Music launched in August 2025 on Merlin and Kobalt licences with claimed commercial clearance [@elevenlabs2025music], Stable Audio's Open model was trained only on Creative Commons audio [@evans2024stableaudioopen], and Mureka reportedly plans structure before generating, on vendor benchmarks only [@kunlun2025mureka].

**DAW-native features and symbolic assistants.** Logic Pro 11 (May 2024) shipped Session Players — Drummer joined by a Bass Player and a Keyboard Player with complexity and intensity controls — which follow the project's Chord Track and, decisively, produce MIDI regions the user can edit [@apple2024logicpro11]. This is the arranger-keyboard idea of F3 reborn inside a DAW; its parameters are a reasonable floor for the studio's compile controls, its gaps the absence of notation, multimodal annotation and arrangement-level reasoning. Ableton Live 12 took a pointedly different position, using machine learning only for retrieval while its MIDI Generators and Transformations are rule-based and fully editable — a reminder, recorded as partial, that transparency is itself a feature many producers trust [@ableton2024live12]. Moises AI Studio generates context-aware stems fitted to the user's audio while declining full songs or vocals [@moises2025aistudio], Splice Create assembles licensed human-made samples rather than generating anything [@splice2024create; @splice2025landr], and WavTool, whose GPT-4 "Conductor" edited projects from natural language, was acquired by Suno in June 2025 and became Suno Studio's basis — the text-to-song leader buying editability [@suno2025wavtool]. On the symbolic side Hookpad Aria is the closest shipping analogue of the studio's loop, serving region-scoped chord, melody or joint infills from the Anticipatory Music Transformer fine-tuned on over 50,000 public TheoryTab analyses, as ordinary editable notation [@donahue2024hookpadaria; @thickstun2024anticipatory]; Composer's Assistant 2 is the open equivalent in REAPER, infilling arbitrary track-measure selections with roughly ten controls [@malandro2024composersassistant2]; MIDI-GPT brings bar-level multi-track infilling with attribute control into Cubase and Ableton under a non-commercial licence [@pasquier2025midigpt]; and Scaler 3 with the Orb and Captain Chords plugins shows the demand for lead-sheet-to-arrangement inside DAWs, on rule-based engines with per-block re-rolls and no notation, both partial [@scaler2025v3; @hexachords2022orb]. AI singers form a separate, symbolic-first category: Synthesizer V Studio 2, ACE Studio, Vocaloid 6 and Kits.ai take note-level input plus lyrics and produce sung vocals with per-note pitch and timbre editing, Kits.ai selling licensed revenue-shared artist voices, so a compiled score's vocal line can be rendered without any text-to-song model [@dreamtonics2025synthv2]. Neutone Morpho runs neural audio models live as plugins with an SDK for training on one's own recordings, making "insert example audio" realisable as a timbre annotation on consented material [@neutone2024morpho], and AbletonMCP heads a wave of LLM agents driving DAWs from natural language [@ahuja2025abletonmcp].

**The empty quadrant.** Against all of this the notation incumbents ship nothing generative: Dorico, Sibelius and MuseScore Studio added proofreading, engraving, scripting and audio work through 2026 with no generative composition, and Finale is discontinued [@steinberg2025dorico6; @avid2026sibelius; @musegroup2025musescore46; @makemusic2024finale]. As of September 2026 a notation-native AI studio has no direct competitor; the nearest neighbours are a lead-sheet web app, two DAW plugins and one DAW feature set, none operating on engraved notation with multimodal annotations. That prompt-only control frustrates users is independently evidenced by the analysis of Suno and Udio prompts, metatags and lyrics from 2024, which documents the elaborate metatag strategies users invent to steer systems offering no structural handles [@casini2025sunoudio].

### 12.2 C2 Law and policy as of September 2026

**The label litigation and its settlements.** On 24 June 2024 Universal, Sony and Warner, coordinated by the RIAA, sued Suno in the District of Massachusetts and Uncharted Labs, Udio's operator, in the Southern District of New York for unlicensed copying of recordings for training, the complaints including side-by-side outputs replicating recordings [@umg2024vsuno]. Plaintiffs added DMCA §1201(a) stream-ripping claims in September 2025, and on 26 May 2026 Universal and Sony moved to assert 61,026 recordings after discovery indicated training on millions of theirs [@umg2024vsuno]. Universal settled with Udio on 29 October 2025, combining compensation with licences and a 2026 walled-garden platform with fingerprinting and artist opt-in [@umg2025udio]; Warner settled with Udio and announced a Stability AI partnership on 19 November 2025, then settled with Suno on 25 November 2025, committing Suno to licensed models in 2026, deprecation of existing ones, artist opt-in and download caps [@wmg2025suno]. Sony and Universal versus Suno remains live and is the bellwether for fair use in music-AI training; the template is licensing in exchange for containment [@suno2026].

**The German rulings changed the analysis.** On 11 November 2025 the Munich Regional Court I held in GEMA v. OpenAI, over nine German song lyrics, that memorisation of lyrics in model weights is a reproduction under §16 UrhG not covered by the text-and-data-mining exception in §44b UrhG and Article 4 of the DSM Directive, that outputting them infringes again, and that liability attaches to OpenAI rather than the prompting user [@lgmunich2025gemaopenai]. On 31 July 2026 the same chamber decided GEMA v. Suno — the judgment is July 2026, the oral hearing originally set for 26 January 2026 was postponed over a recusal motion and heard on 9 March 2026, which is why January dates are sometimes reported for this case — over outputs resembling well-known catalogue songs, finding the works effectively stored in Suno's models so that memorisation amounts to reproduction, that stream-ripping from YouTube circumvented technical protection measures, that §44b does not apply where works are retained in reproducible form, and that Suno rather than prompting users is liable for infringing outputs generated in Germany [@lgmunich2026gemasuno]. This first European judgment on the merits against a music generator extends the lyrics logic to music itself, with two consequences for any model distributed in the EU: memorisation audits become mandatory, for which MiRA is the open baseline, and clean training data becomes the only reliable defence [@batlleroca2024mira].

**United States copyrightability points the other way, in the studio's favour.** The Copyright Office's *Copyright and Artificial Intelligence* series — Part 1 on digital replicas (2024), Part 2 on copyrightability (29 January 2025) and Part 3 on training in pre-publication form (May 2025), the final Part 3 not confirmed published as of September 2026 and dates drawn from secondary sources — sets out the operative distinction. Part 2 holds that prompts alone confer no authorship, while human-authored expressive inputs, creative selection and arrangement, and modifications of AI output are protectable to the extent of the human contribution; Part 3 treats training as prima facie reproduction, unlikely to be fair use where outputs compete with the training works or the data was obtained unlawfully [@usco2025part2]. Thaler v. Perlmutter fixed the floor: the D.C. Circuit affirmed in March 2025 that the Copyright Act requires a human author, and certiorari was denied on 2 March 2026, leaving undecided how much human contribution suffices [@thaler2025perlmutter]. Together these lines yield the conclusion that matters most for design: human-authored, human-edited notation is protectable, prompt-only output generally is not, and a provenance log may generate evidence of authorship as a by-product of ordinary operation [@usco2025part2; @thaler2025perlmutter; @micchi2021ikeepcounting].

**Transparency and likeness regimes.** Under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models have applied since 2 August 2025, with enforcement powers from August 2026: technical documentation, a copyright policy respecting machine-readable opt-outs under Article 4(3) of the DSM Directive, and a published "sufficiently detailed summary" of training content on the Commission's July 2025 template, from neither of the latter two of which open-source models are exempt [@eu2024aiact]. For a studio trained only on public-domain, Creative Commons and consented data that summary is trivial to produce. Tennessee's ELVIS Act, which took effect in 2024, was the first United States state law extending the right of publicity to voice including simulations, with liability both for unauthorised use and for distributing tools whose primary purpose is producing such replicas [@tennessee2024elvis; @dreamtonics2025synthv2]. Concord Music Group v. Anthropic, filed October 2023 over lyrics and recorded as partial for 2026, tests output-side infringement for language models [@concord2023anthropic], and Sturm and colleagues' 2019 framing of the open questions — authorship, training-data legitimacy, the status of style — anticipated most of this [@sturm2019openquestions].

### 12.3 C3 Ethics, provenance and disclosure

**Consent standards emerged from inside the industry.** Ed Newton-Rex resigned as VP Audio at Stability AI in November 2023 over its fair-use position on training and launched the non-profit Fairly Trained in January 2024, certifying models trained only on licensed, public-domain or consented data, early certifications including Beatoven, Soundful, Kits.ai and LANDR [@newtonrex2024fairlytrained]. The "Statement on AI training" of 22 October 2024 — one sentence declaring unlicensed use of creative works for training an unjust threat to creators' livelihoods — launched with roughly 10,500 signatories and later exceeded 50,000; both entries are partial [@newtonrex2024statement]. Spawning built the opt-out infrastructure — Have I Been Trained? over LAION-5B, the Do Not Train registry and ai.txt — while its founders produced Holly+, a model of Holly Herndon's own voice offered for others to use, as consent-based training in artistic practice [@spawning2022haveibeentrained; @herndon2021hollyplus].

**Disclosure has become platform infrastructure.** On 25 September 2025 Spotify announced an impersonation policy permitting removal of unauthorised AI voice clones, a spam filter against mass uploads and sub-30-second bait tracks — 75 million "spammy" tracks removed in the preceding year — and support for the DDEX AI-disclosure standard so distributors can credit AI's role in vocals, instrumentation or production [@spotify2025aipolicy]. Deezer's telemetry charts the scale: fully-AI tracks rose from roughly 10 per cent of daily deliveries in January 2025 to over half at peak in June 2026, around 90,000 per day; they have been labelled to listeners since June 2026 and excluded from recommendation, and while they draw only 1 to 3 per cent of streams, up to 85 per cent of those streams were identified as fraudulent in 2025 and demonetised [@deezer2026aistats]. Watermarking supplies the technical complement — Google applies SynthID to all Lyria outputs [@google2025lyria2], and research on watermarking *training* data tests whether audio watermarks survive into a generator's outputs [@epple2024watermarking] — while MiRA audits in the other direction, using audio similarity metrics to detect exact replication above roughly 10 per cent [@batlleroca2024mira]. Why this matters commercially is illustrated by United States v. Michael Smith, who pleaded guilty on 19 March 2026 to wire-fraud conspiracy of $8.09 million after streaming hundreds of thousands of AI-generated tracks through roughly 1,040 bot accounts, detected by the Mechanical Licensing Collective rather than by the platforms [@usdoj2024smith].

**What not to do, and the position that follows.** The musescore.com scrape is the negative exemplar: an unofficial dump of scores, metadata and user data harvested through the site's public API and distributed over IPFS so it could not be removed, unlicensed, largely user arrangements of copyrighted works and in breach of the platform's terms — used, without disclosure, by several symbolic-model papers [@xmader2021musescore]; PDMX demonstrates the clean alternative within the same source material [@long2024pdmx]. The studio's ethical position is therefore implementable rather than aspirational: outputs are symbolic and human-authored, so authorship rests with the composer and is protectable [@usco2025part2]; computation runs locally where possible, so the composer's material is not a training contribution by default; training data is restricted to public-domain, Creative Commons and consented sources with per-file licence metadata retained, making a Fairly Trained-style certification and an EU AI Act training summary achievable [@newtonrex2024fairlytrained; @eu2024aiact; @long2024pdmx]; vocal rendering, if offered, uses consented singing-synthesis voices [@dreamtonics2025synthv2; @tennessee2024elvis]; and provenance is emitted by design, as DDEX AI credits and as a compile history recording which material was human-authored and which annotations produced which generated regions [@spotify2025aipolicy; @micchi2021ikeepcounting]. Residual risks should be stated rather than concealed: stylistic imitation of living composers remains possible, transcribed-from-audio corpora carry greyer provenance than PDMX, and any bundled audio renderer inherits its own training-data questions [@bradshaw2025ariamidi; @evans2024stableaudioopen].

### 12.4 C4 Economics and creator attitudes

The economic studies commissioned by collecting societies frame the stakes in revenue terms. Goldmedia's *AI and Music* study for GEMA and SACEM, published in January 2024 on a survey of roughly 15,000 members, projected the generative music market at about $3 billion by 2028 and put 27 per cent of creators' revenues at risk by that date, with 71 per cent judging risks to outweigh opportunities and 95 per cent wanting disclosure of training data [@goldmedia2024gemasacem]. CISAC's study with PMP Strategy (December 2024) modelled generative output at roughly €64 billion by 2028 with creators risking a modelled 24 per cent of revenues through substitution and unlicensed training [@cisac2024pmp]. Both are partially verified. The attitudinal picture is measured more precisely: APRA AMCOS's report of 19 August 2024, surveying over 4,200 members, found 54 per cent agreeing that AI can *assist* the creative process against 82 per cent fearing for their livelihood, with 97 per cent wanting policy action and training-data disclosure, 95 per cent demanding permission before use, 23 per cent of revenue at risk by 2028 and 89 per cent of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander creators fearing cultural appropriation [@apraamcos2024ai]. Listeners cannot police the boundary themselves: the Deezer and Ipsos survey of 9,000 adults across eight countries found 97 per cent unable to distinguish fully-AI tracks in blind listening, 80 per cent wanting labelling and 65 per cent opposing training on copyrighted material [@deezer2025ipsos] — though detectability is not fixed, since in a blind crossover design over real Suno outputs listeners' accuracy rose causally when pairs were made more similar [@figueiredo2025echoes].

What professional composers ask for, when asked directly, reads as a specification of the studio. Newman, Morris and Lee's ISMIR 2023 study of six professionals across classical, jazz, film, game, interactive-media and electroacoustic practice — 60- to 90-minute semi-structured interviews, two-coder analysis — produced four design implications: preserve creative control by confining AI to ideation rather than intentional decisions; provide interoperability through MIDI, WAV and MusicXML export or integration into software already in use; separate "influences" from "mechanisms"; and prefer open-source, explainable models over black boxes [@newman2023perceptions]. The same preferences recur in the folk-rnn practitioner work and Sony CSL's eighteen-month embedded study, which add that tool-switching kills adoption and that documented human selection and modification is what practitioners regard as constituting ownership [@sturm2019machinefolk; @deruty2022sonycsl; @micchi2021ikeepcounting]. The studio's positioning is thus not a moral posture adopted at cost but the configuration these studies describe as acceptable.

### 12.5 Implications for the studio

The competitive conclusion is that the empty quadrant is real and defensible. Every 2026 product either generates opaque audio from a prompt and is now enclosed by licensing settlements, or generates editable symbolic material inside a DAW at clip or bar granularity, or engraves notation with no generative capability at all [@suno2026; @udio2025; @apple2024logicpro11; @malandro2024composersassistant2; @steinberg2025dorico6; @avid2026sibelius; @musegroup2025musescore46]; nothing compiles engraved notation from human-authored material plus multimodal annotations. Hookpad Aria validates the interaction primitive — select a region, ask, accept or edit — on a small, consented, functional-harmony dataset, showing it can be productised without a foundation model or a legally encumbered corpus [@donahue2024hookpadaria; @thickstun2024anticipatory], and Suno Studio and WavTool supply the reverse confirmation: the leaders of the rejected paradigm spent two years acquiring editability, arriving at MIDI editing inside a workstation whose generator still cannot read it [@suno2026; @suno2025wavtool].

The legal conclusion is that the studio's architecture is also its legal strategy. Because outputs are notation the composer wrote, selected, arranged and edited, they fall on the protectable side of the Copyright Office's Part 2 analysis and the Thaler rule, whereas prompt-only outputs generally do not [@usco2025part2; @thaler2025perlmutter]; and because training is confined to public-domain, Creative Commons and consented corpora, the memorisation-as-reproduction holding of the Munich rulings is not merely survivable but inapplicable, while the EU AI Act's training-content summary becomes a differentiator rather than a burden [@lgmunich2025gemaopenai; @lgmunich2026gemasuno; @eu2024aiact; @long2024pdmx]. Three features follow and should be first-class: a provenance log recording authorship and edit history at region granularity, serving at once as authorship record, DDEX AI-credit source and debugging aid; a memorisation audit in the evaluation harness for any released model; and per-file licence metadata carried through the data pipeline [@micchi2021ikeepcounting; @spotify2025aipolicy; @batlleroca2024mira].

Two gaps remain. No product in this landscape emits provenance a composer could use to demonstrate authorship, and no legal or platform regime specifies what such a record should contain, so a well-designed compile history is a contribution to practice as much as to software. And the economic literature measures what creators fear and want in aggregate while offering no longitudinal evidence on whether an assistive, symbolic, provenance-emitting tool changes professional output or income [@apraamcos2024ai; @goldmedia2024gemasacem; @cisac2024pmp] — evidence the studio is positioned to generate, which is the argument in E2 for building telemetry and longitudinal study design in from the first release [@newman2023perceptions; @deruty2022sonycsl].
