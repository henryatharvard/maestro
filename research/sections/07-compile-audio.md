## 7. Compile II: audio rendering and audio-domain generation (S3.8–S3.10)

The symbolic compile stages surveyed in S3.1–S3.7 end with an editable score or an expressive MIDI performance; a composer still needs to *hear* it, increasingly as a produced arrangement rather than a General MIDI mock-up. This section covers the audio-domain literature the studio must both borrow from and define itself against, and it is organised deliberately out of taxonomic order. S3.9, text-to-music and full-song generation, comes first because it is the paradigm the studio inverts and because the 2023–2026 trajectory of its commercial and open-weights systems is itself evidence about what musicians want. S3.8 isolates the thinner line in which an audio model accepts *symbolic* conditions (chords, melody, MIDI, time-varying curves) or renders notation through interpretable synthesis, which is the studio's actual rendering requirement. S3.10 asks whether music-understanding models can serve as compile-time critics. Works are coded throughout on domain (D1), control granularity (D3), editability of the intermediate representation (D7) and openness of weights and training data (D10).

### 7.1 S3.9 Text-to-music and full-song audio generation (the paradigm to invert)

**Foundations (2020–2024).** Jukebox compressed 44.1 kHz audio through a three-level VQ-VAE and modelled the top level with autoregressive Transformers of up to 5B parameters, producing minutes-long songs with singing conditioned on artist, genre and unaligned lyrics; it offered no symbolic conditioning, no editing beyond primed continuation, and needed hours of compute per minute of audio [@dhariwal2020jukebox; @castellon2021codified]. The 2023 wave replaced raw-audio autoregression with neural codecs and latent diffusion. MusicLM cascaded a MuLan music–text embedding, semantic and acoustic tokens to generate 24 kHz music from text, released MusicCaps (5.5k expert-captioned 10-s clips) as the de-facto benchmark and, notably, could transform a *hummed or whistled melody* into the described style [@agostinelli2023musiclm]; Noise2Music introduced LLM pseudo-labelling of training captions [@huang2023noise2music]. Meta's MusicGen collapsed the cascade into a single-stage Transformer over interleaved EnCodec streams (300M–3.3B, 32 kHz, ~30 s) conditioned on text and a chromagram of a reference melody; its MIT code and CC-BY-NC 4.0 weights made it the most-extended open backbone, underlying Coco-Mulla, Instruct-MusicGen, MusicGen-Style and MusicGen-Stem [@copet2023musicgen; @lin2023cocomulla; @zhang2025instructmusicgen; @rouard2024musicgenstyle; @rouard2025musicgenstem]. MAGNeT showed that non-autoregressive masked generation over the same tokens ran roughly 7× faster, and its re-mask-and-redecode procedure is inherently local-editable [@ziv2024magnet].

The diffusion branch supplied the editing primitives. AudioLDM ran latent diffusion in a VAE-compressed mel space conditioned on CLAP embeddings and demonstrated zero-shot inpainting, style transfer and super-resolution without task-specific training [@liu2023audioldm; @wu2023clap]; AudioLDM 2 added a "language of audio" intermediate representation into which any conditioning modality is translated before rendering, a pattern for one renderer serving many condition types [@liu2024audioldm2]. Stable Audio 2.0 generated 4 min 45 s stereo tracks with explicit *timing conditioning* (start time and total length) and audio-to-audio initialisation from a user's clip, but its weights, trained on licensed AudioSparx data, were never released [@evans2024longform]. Its open sibling Stable Audio Open (≈1.2B parameters, up to 47 s) was trained only on Creative Commons audio from Freesound and the Free Music Archive (≈486k recordings) under the Stability AI Community License, making it the cleanest-provenance base for later controllable adapters [@evans2024stableaudioopen; @tsai2025musecontrollite; @hou2024melodycontrolnet; @floresgarcia2025sketch2sound]. JEN-1 and MusicFlow each showed that generation, inpainting and continuation can be one model, the former by mixing bidirectional and causal training, the latter by masked-prediction training of cascaded flow-matching networks; neither released weights [@li2023jen1; @prajwal2024musicflow]. Riffusion, a 2022 project that fine-tuned Stable Diffusion on spectrogram images, matters as a case study: it became the conversational, multi-turn ProducerAI in 2025, was acquired by Google in February 2026 and re-emerged in April 2026 as Google Flow Music on Lyria 3 with section-level "replace and extend" [@forsgren2022riffusion].

**Commercial full-song platforms and their drift toward editing.** These systems are the archetype the studio rejects: one prompt yields a finished mix, the human is prompt author and curator, and the intermediate representation is opaque audio (D7). Their feature histories nevertheless record sustained user pressure toward region- and part-level control. Suno's model line ran from v3 (February 2024, 2-min clips) through v4 and v4.5 to v5 (September 2025) and v5.5 (March 2026, with user-trainable "Custom Models") [@suno2026]. Around the generator it accreted Audio Input (June 2024), stem separation (July 2024, later 12-stem export), Covers (keep melody, change style), Replace Section (regenerate a time range, October 2024), Personas, Remaster and Add Vocals/Add Instrumental; in September 2025 it launched Suno Studio, a browser "generative audio workstation" with a multitrack timeline and per-section regeneration, and in August 2026 Studio 2.0 added MIDI import, recording and editing with a wavetable synthesizer [@suno2026]. The generator itself remained prompt- and audio-driven; MIDI lives inside the DAW, not as a generation condition. The Warner Music Group settlement of 25 November 2025 committed Suno to licensed models in 2026, deprecation of existing models, artist opt-in, a non-downloadable free tier and monthly download caps, reported to have taken effect on 3 September 2026 at 7 lifetime downloads on Free, 20 a month on Pro and 60 on Premier — figures documented in Suno's subscription help pages rather than its release notes, and so carried here as reported rather than verified [@wmg2025suno; @suno2026]. Udio, launched in April 2024, generated ~30-s segments extended forwards and backwards into songs and carried the earliest mainstream instance recorded in this corpus of *audio inpainting* of a user-selected region [@udio2025]. After settling with Universal on 29 October 2025 it disabled downloads and announced a 2026 subscription "walled garden" in which creations remain inside the platform, fingerprinted and filtered [@umg2025udio; @udio2025]. Both trajectories show the same two facts: users demanded region editing, and licensed audio generation arrived bundled with constrained ownership of the output.

Google's line is the most studio-like of the closed systems. Lyria powered the Music AI Sandbox, co-designed with musicians in the YouTube Music AI Incubator, whose verbs are *Create* (text plus optional lyrics on a timeline with key and tempo), *Extend* and *Edit* (transform a whole clip or a selected region, fill gaps, blend transitions); multitrack views and inpainting arrived in October 2024 and Lyria 2 with 48 kHz stereo output in April 2025, all outputs SynthID-watermarked and trained, Google states, on music it and YouTube "have the right to use" [@google2025lyria2; @googledeepmind2025sandbox]. Lyria RealTime, exposed through the Gemini API in mid-2025, is a block-autoregressive streaming model steered by weighted text prompts plus BPM, key, density and brightness with a reported control latency of at most 2 s; its PromptDJ-MIDI demo maps physical MIDI controllers to those *parameters*, not to notes [@google2025lyriarealtime]. Lyria 3 (February 2026) and Lyria 3.5 (mid-2026, reportedly 3-minute songs) power the Gemini app and Flow Music [@google2025lyriarealtime; @forsgren2022riffusion]. ElevenLabs Music (August 2025) shipped lyric editing throughout a track, regeneration of a verse, chorus or bridge, 2–6-stem export and API inpainting, positioned as trained on licensed data [@elevenlabs2025music]. Mureka reportedly marketed its O1 model in March 2025 as a "chain-of-thought" music model that plans structure before generating; these claims rest on vendor material [@kunlun2025mureka; @mureka2025]. Adobe's Project Music GenAI Control (February 2024, with UC San Diego and CMU) framed the problem as "generate, then edit with musical parameters" (tempo, structure, intensity, reference melody, section remix) but never shipped; it productised Music ControlNet and DITTO (S3.8) [@adobe2024musicgenaicontrol; @wu2024musiccontrolnet; @novack2024ditto]. Stable Audio 2.5 (September 2025) generated 3-minute multi-part tracks in under 2 s with API inpainting and audio-to-audio; its adversarial relativistic-contrastive (ARC) post-training also yielded the open-weights Stable Audio Open Small at roughly 75 ms for 12 s of stereo on an H100 [@novack2025arc]. None of these platforms publishes model details, benchmarks or user studies; the evidence level (D9) is product claims.

**Open-weights song models.** Between January 2025 and early 2026 permissively licensed lyrics-to-song models made the commercial paradigm reproducible on consumer hardware. YuE (released January 2025) is a LLaMA-2-based two-stage LM (7B semantic, 1B acoustic) generating up to 5 min of multilingual song with *track-decoupled* prediction that interleaves vocal and accompaniment tokens and in-context style transfer from a 30-s audio prompt; it is Apache-2.0 but heavy (24 GB VRAM minimum, ~360 s per 30 s on an RTX 4090) [@yuan2025yue]. DiffRhythm (ACL 2025) is a non-autoregressive latent DiT producing 4 min 45 s songs in about 10 s from *timestamped lyrics* and a text or audio style prompt, Apache-2.0 on 8 GB of VRAM, with extension and editing added in v1.2 [@ning2025diffrhythm]. SongGen (ICML 2025) is a 1.3B single-stage autoregressive Transformer with a *dual-track* mode emitting separate vocal and accompaniment streams, trained on a fully open ~2k h pipeline, Apache-2.0, limited to 30-s English songs [@liu2025songgen]. HeartMuLa (December 2025–February 2026) bundles a 3B open music LM with a 12.5 Hz codec, a lyric transcriber and a CLAP model under Apache-2.0, generating songs of up to 240 s at a real-time factor near 1; its claimed parity with Suno is self-reported [@heartmula2026].

ACE-Step deserves the closest reading because it is the strongest open candidate for the studio's arranger and renderer. ACE-Step 1.0 (ACE Studio and StepFun, May 2025) is a flow model over Sana's deep-compression autoencoder latents with a linear Transformer, aligned through REPA to MERT features; it generated up to 4 min of music with vocals in about 20 s on an A100, reportedly some 15× faster than LM-based systems, and shipped a menu of editing operations rather than one-shot generation: variations, *repainting* (local regeneration of a time range), lyric editing that keeps melody and accompaniment, lyric-to-vocal, singing-to-accompaniment, voice cloning and remixing through LoRA controllers [@gong2025acestep; @li2024mert]. ACE-Step 1.5 (technical report January–February 2026; 4B XL release April 2026) restructured the system as a hybrid *LM planner plus diffusion Transformer*: a 0.6B–4B language model converts the request into a "song blueprint" (metadata, lyrics, captions, via chain-of-thought) that conditions a DiT decoder; intrinsic RL alignment removes the external reward model; an 8-step turbo variant renders a full song in under 2 s on an A100 and under 10 s on an RTX 3090; memory ranges from under 4–6 GB to 24 GB or more; backends include CUDA, ROCm, Apple MLX and CPU; and it ships a REST and Python API and a VST3 plugin [@gong2026acestep15]. New capabilities include cover generation, repainting, vocal-to-accompaniment, track separation, multi-track layering, lyric-structure control, BPM/key/time-signature control, over 50 languages and LoRA fine-tuning from a few songs. The licence is MIT. Two gaps matter: there is no MIDI or chord input to the generator, and no training-data statement, so provenance is unknown (D10). Its planner is in effect a compiler front-end from intent to blueprint; a notation-first studio would replace that planner with the composer's score and annotations and retain the decoder.

**Stem-level "listen and respond" generation.** A distinct strand frames generation not as producing a song but as adding a part that fits what is already there, the unit of co-creation closest to arranging. SingSong source-separated a large corpus into (vocal, instrumental) pairs and trained AudioLM-style conditional generation to accompany a new a-cappella input; listeners preferred its accompaniments to retrieval baselines in a reported ~66% of pairwise comparisons [@donahue2023singsong]. StemGen (ICASSP 2024) is a non-autoregressive masked Transformer that takes an existing multi-stem context and generates one new stem of a requested category, evaluated by key, tempo and chroma coherence with the context; no weights were released [@parker2024stemgen]. Multi-Source Diffusion Models (ICLR 2024 oral) learned the *joint* score of bass, drums, guitar and piano on Slakh2100 so that a single model performs generation, separation and *source imputation*, filling in missing stems given the others [@mariani2024msdm; @manilow2019slakh]. Diff-A-Riff (Sony CSL, ISMIR 2024) generated a single 48 kHz accompaniment track to fit a given mix, steerable by a CLAP audio reference and/or text and designed for DAW workflows [@nistal2024diffariff]. MusicGen-Stem trained one codec per stem (bass, drums, other) so that one stem can be regenerated while the others are held fixed [@rouard2025musicgenstem], and Instruct-MusicGen added fusion modules to frozen MusicGen (about 8% extra parameters, 5k steps) so that it follows "add, remove or separate a stem" instructions, matching task-specific models on Slakh and MoisesDB [@zhang2025instructmusicgen]. Stemphonic, which generates a variable set of synchronised stems in one pass under per-stem activity patterns, is the arrangement-oriented endpoint of this line (S3.5) [@wu2026stemphonic]. In D2 terms these systems occupy the *response/accompaniment* framing, in which the human's material is a hard constraint, which is why they rather than the full-song generators are the natural audio analogue of the studio's compile step.

### 7.2 S3.8 Audio rendering and symbolic-conditioned audio generation

The studio's rendering requirement is narrower and harder than text-to-music: given notation, chords or MIDI, produce audio that honours them, with enough control to re-render one region after an edit. Only a handful of models accept structured musical input. JASCO (Hebrew University and Meta, ISMIR 2024) is a flow-matching text-to-music model that takes time-aligned *chord progressions* and a *melody salience matrix* as symbolic controls, plus a separated drum track and a full-mix embedding as audio controls, through information-bottleneck layers with temporal blurring; a human study found quality comparable to baselines with "significantly better and more versatile controls" [@tal2024jasco]. It is the clearest open example of lead-sheet-style conditioning of an audio model, but it produces roughly 10-s clips at 32 kHz under CC-BY-NC weights. Coco-Mulla fine-tuned under 4% of MusicGen's parameters on fewer than 300 songs to accept chord progressions, drum MIDI and pitch content alongside text, a cheap recipe for teaching an existing audio LM to read a composer's chords [@lin2023cocomulla]. Mustango (NAACL 2024) injected chords, beats, key and tempo parsed from the prompt through a music-domain-informed UNet module and released MusicBench (52k music–text pairs with MIR-extracted theory captions), improving chord, beat, key and tempo accuracy over MusicGen and AudioLDM 2 [@melechovsky2024mustango].

A second family conditions on time-varying *curves*, which maps directly onto what a composer might draw over a passage. Music ControlNet (CMU and Adobe, TASLP 2024) attached ControlNet-style adapters to a spectrogram diffusion model for melody (chroma), dynamics (loudness) and rhythm (beat and downbeat) curves; crucially, controls may be *partially specified in time*, pinning melody for bars 1–4 and leaving the rest free, and the model was 49% more faithful to input melodies than MusicGen with 35× fewer parameters and 11× less training data, though no code was released [@wu2024musiccontrolnet]. MuseControlLite (ICML 2025) reproduced these controls as 85M-parameter decoupled cross-attention adapters on Stable Audio Open, using rotary positional embeddings for time-varying conditions (melody accuracy 56.6% to 61.1%), and added native audio inpainting and outpainting with 6.75× fewer trainable parameters than a Stable Audio Open ControlNet; open and CC-trained, it is arguably the best starting point for a studio prototype's audio stage [@tsai2025musecontrollite; @evans2024stableaudioopen]. The Melody+Text ControlNet for Stable Audio's DiT reportedly used a top-k constant-Q melody representation with curriculum learning to balance melody against text [@hou2024melodycontrolnet]. Sketch2Sound, which extracts loudness, brightness and pitch curves from a vocal imitation with a tunable looseness, belongs to the same family and is treated under S1.2 [@floresgarcia2025sketch2sound].

DITTO reframes all of these as one mechanism. Rather than training adapters, it optimises the initial noise latents of a frozen text-to-music diffusion model through any differentiable feature-matching loss, obtaining inpainting, outpainting, looping, intensity-curve, melody and musical-structure (self-similarity) control from one model and outperforming training-based baselines including Music ControlNet; DITTO-2 distilled the model and optimised through a one-step surrogate for a 10–20× speed-up, faster than real time, and added targets such as maximising CLAP text adherence [@novack2024ditto; @wu2023clap]. For the studio this is the general "annotation → constraint" mechanism: any composer annotation with a computable target (a hummed contour, a dynamics sketch, a form diagram) becomes a loss on a frozen renderer without retraining per annotation type. Text2FX applies the same logic to interpretable production parameters, optimising differentiable EQ and reverb so that the processed audio's CLAP embedding matches a prompt (S4.3) [@chu2025text2fx].

The most composer-aligned rendering lineage remains DDSP. DDSP made synthesizer and effect modules differentiable so that networks output interpretable parameters (fundamental frequency, loudness, harmonic distribution) instead of waveforms, enabling independent pitch and loudness control and timbre transfer with small models [@engel2020ddsp]. MIDI-DDSP (ICLR 2022) built a three-level hierarchy on it, notes → per-note performance parameters (vibrato, dynamics, articulation, brightness) → synthesis parameters → audio, for monophonic URMP instruments under Apache-2.0; a user may write notes, edit an expression curve or adjust synthesis directly, or let learned priors fill in, the clearest published instance of a compiler with editable intermediate representations (D7 multi-level editable) [@wu2022mididdsp]. DDSP-VST packaged the approach as MIDI-playable neural synthesizer and re-timbring effect plugins with a trainer that builds a personal timbre from "a couple minutes of audio," and Tone Transfer put humming-to-instrument in the browser (S1.2) [@magenta2022ddspvst; @magenta2020tonetransfer]. Its limitation is monophony. MusicHiFi, reportedly a cascade of three GANs for vocoding, bandwidth extension and mono-to-stereo upmixing, addresses a back-end concern: many controllable models output 16–32 kHz mono, and a modular finishing stage can lift them to 44.1 kHz stereo [@zhu2024musichifi].

Finally, real-time MIDI-steerable audio arrived in 2026. Magenta RealTime 2 (June 2026) moved the open "live music model" from 2-s chunks to 40-ms frames with roughly 200 ms control latency on Apple Silicon (230M and 2.4B models) and accepts *MIDI notes* (Auto-Strum and precise-onset modes) alongside text and audio style prompts; it was trained on about 71k hours of mostly instrumental stock music paired with MT3-inferred MIDI labels, the only open audio model in this corpus whose training pairs audio with transcribed symbolic data [@lyria2025livemusic; @gardner2022mt3]. Its short context precludes long-form structure, but it is the first credible open building block for rendering or jamming on the studio's symbolic material inside a plugin (S5.3).

### 7.3 S3.10 Music-understanding models as compile-time critics

A compiler that emits audio needs a way to check its output: did the rendering keep the key and tempo, follow the chords, land the annotated dynamics? The music-understanding literature offers encoders, text–audio embeddings and audio-language models, but none is yet reliable enough to be trusted silently. The encoder layer is the most mature. JukeMIR showed in 2021 that Jukebox's intermediate representations transferred to tagging, genre, key and emotion tasks with linear probes and seeded the Sheet Sage melody transcriber (S1.1) [@castellon2021codified; @donahue2022sheetsage]. MERT (ICLR 2024) trained HuBERT-style masked prediction against an RVQ-VAE acoustic teacher and a constant-Q *musical* teacher for pitch and harmony, at 95M and 330M parameters, reaching state-of-the-art or competitive results across 14 MIR tasks; its CC-BY-SA 4.0 weights became the default backbone for audio-side annotation and are used inside MU-LLaMA, MusiLingo and ACE-Step [@li2024mert; @liu2023mullama; @deng2024musilingo; @gong2025acestep]. MuQ replaced MERT's teachers with a light Mel residual vector quantiser and beat MERT and MusicFM with only 0.9k hours of pretraining; its companion MuQ-MuLan is an open contrastive music–text model, the missing open analogue of MusicLM's MuLan [@zhu2025muq; @agostinelli2023musiclm]. LAION-CLAP, contrastive text–audio encoders trained on LAION-Audio-630K, is the de-facto text conditioner (AudioLDM, Stable Audio Open, Diff-A-Riff) and evaluation metric, and because it defines a shared text–audio space it lets text annotations act as loss functions in DITTO-2 and Text2FX [@wu2023clap; @liu2023audioldm; @nistal2024diffariff; @novack2024ditto; @chu2025text2fx].

The audio-LLM layer, where critique would happen, is weaker. MU-LLaMA (ICASSP 2024) joined MERT to LLaMA through an adapter for music question answering and captioning and introduced MusicQA [@liu2023mullama]. LLark (Spotify, ICML 2024) paired a Jukebox-5B encoder with Llama-2 on instruction data auto-generated from open datasets' annotations, producing key, tempo and instrumentation facts conversationally with high human agreement on captions; training code was released, weights were not [@gardner2024llark]. MusiLingo reportedly used a single projection layer between MERT and Vicuna [@deng2024musilingo]. SALMONN (ICLR 2024) fused Whisper and BEATs encoders through a Q-Former into Vicuna, handling speech, audio events and music in one model, a candidate front-end for parsing spoken and hummed annotations [@tang2024salmonn]. General models such as Qwen2-Audio (7B, Apache-2.0), Qwen2.5-Omni and Gemini 2.5 describe genre, instruments and mood and roughly estimate tempo, and Gemini is what Google pairs with Lyria, but they are not music-specialised; only Qwen2-Audio's report was verified directly [@chu2024qwen2audio]. OpenMU (Sony) is the one benchmark mixing the modalities the studio cares about: OpenMU-Bench (≈1M examples) covers captioning and reasoning but also lyrics, *ABC-notation* understanding and tool use, and the OpenMU model outperformed MU-LLaMA on it [@zhao2024openmu].

Two results temper the picture. MuChoMusic (ISMIR 2024) built 1,187 human-validated multiple-choice questions on 644 tracks from MusicCaps and Song Describer, spanning theory, instrumentation, structure and cultural reasoning, and evaluated five open audio LLMs including MU-LLaMA, MusiLingo, SALMONN and Qwen-Audio; the central finding was *over-reliance on the language modality*, models often answering without using the audio [@weck2024muchomusic; @manco2023songdescriber]. The second is the field's one user-centred data point. Ronchini et al. (CMMR 2025) had 17 music producers from seven countries use a tool combining MusicGen with HT-Demucs six-stem separation, followed by interviews and thematic analysis; the themes were "creative misalignment" between intent and output, integration pain concentrated on *tempo, key and beat alignment*, strong demand for control and editability, and a split between ideation (94% would use it) and production (few would) [@ronchini2025ttmuserstudy; @copet2023musicgen]. The alignment problems named by working producers are precisely the ones symbolic conditioning solves by construction, and the field's evaluation practice, FAD, CLAP score and small MOS panels, does not measure them (E4) [@kilgour2019fad; @chung2025kad].

### 7.4 Implications for the studio

The first lesson is that the paradigm being inverted has been inverting itself. Every commercial platform surveyed began as prompt-to-song and added, under user pressure, stems, section replacement, covers, personas, timelines and finally MIDI editing; Google moved from a Sandbox with region edit and extend to real-time steering to conversational section-level refinement; the open ACE-Step line shipped repaint, lyric-edit, cover and vocal-to-accompaniment operations and a planner that turns intent into a structured blueprint before decoding [@suno2026; @google2025lyria2; @forsgren2022riffusion; @gong2026acestep15]. What none of them did is make notation the source of truth or accept symbolic material as a generation condition; Suno's MIDI lives inside its DAW, and Udio's licensed platform keeps the output inside a walled garden [@suno2026; @udio2025]. The studio's differentiators are therefore concrete: compile from the composer's score and annotations, expose editable intermediate representations in the MIDI-DDSP sense, emit portable notation, MIDI and stems, and state the provenance of any audio model it ships [@wu2022mididdsp; @evans2024stableaudioopen].

The second lesson is that the rendering stage the studio needs exists only as research-grade pieces, and the pieces can be assembled. Symbolic conditioning of audio is demonstrated by JASCO, Coco-Mulla, Mustango and Music ControlNet; native region inpainting by MuseControlLite, JEN-1, MusicFlow and ACE-Step; part-at-a-time generation by SingSong, MSDM, Diff-A-Riff, MusicGen-Stem and Stemphonic; training-free imposition of arbitrary computable constraints by DITTO; per-note editable synthesis by MIDI-DDSP; and MIDI-steerable real-time rendering by Magenta RealTime 2 [@tal2024jasco; @lin2023cocomulla; @tsai2025musecontrollite; @mariani2024msdm; @novack2024ditto; @wu2022mididdsp; @lyria2025livemusic]. A plausible open stack is a CC-trained base (Stable Audio Open) with MuseControlLite-style adapters for curves, a Coco-Mulla-style adapter for chords and drum MIDI, DITTO for ad-hoc annotation constraints, per-stem regeneration, and an ACE-Step decoder where vocals or full-band production are required, with weight licences checked separately from code licences because MusicGen-family weights are non-commercial. The gaps are equally concrete: no open model renders polyphonic notation with MIDI-DDSP's level of control; symbolic-conditioned models produce 10–47-s clips; Magenta RealTime 2 has no long-form structure; and no verified model accepts a score, as opposed to chords or a melody curve, as its condition.

The third lesson concerns the critic. MERT, MuQ and CLAP are dependable feature extractors for key, tempo, chord and text-adherence probes, but MuChoMusic's finding that audio LLMs answer from text rather than sound means their judgments cannot close the loop unsupervised [@li2024mert; @zhu2025muq; @weck2024muchomusic]. A symbolic-first studio has an advantage that audio-first products lack: it can verify a rendering against the score it compiled from, using transcription and MIR probes on the output and reserving the LLM for phrasing the discrepancy back to the composer (S4.4). Ronchini et al.'s producers asked for exactly this alignment guarantee, and their study remains the only user evidence in an otherwise metric-driven field, a gap the studio's own evaluation programme (E1, E4) should fill [@ronchini2025ttmuserstudy].
