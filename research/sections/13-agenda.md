## 13. Gap analysis and a research agenda

The survey's most useful output is not the inventory but the shape of what is missing. The gaps below were derived by intersecting two things: the density of the taxonomy's nodes — where the corpus is thin — with the studio's own design requirements, which are stated as positions on the ten coding dimensions. A node that is crowded but irrelevant (text-to-song, 23 primary entries) is not a gap; a node that is thin *and* load-bearing is. Each gap names the nearest prior art to build on, so that none of them starts from nothing.

### 13.1 Gaps that are candidate contributions

**G1. Annotations as a compilable instruction set on notation.** *(Node S2.4: four primary entries. Dimensions D4, D6, D7.)* This is the central gap and the one the studio is best positioned to close. The constituent parts are all present in the corpus and never assembled: typed, musical-time-anchored annotation records in MEI, Dezrann and RomanText [@pugin2014verovio; @giraud2018dezrann; @gotham2019wheninrome]; free-form ink, images and audio placed on and between staves without an engraver enforcing syntax, as in EuterPen [@cavez2025euterpen]; a reference grammar that decomposes a mixed natural-language-and-sketch request into temporal, spatial, operation and parameter components with per-modality resolvers and inspectable suggestions, as in ExpressEdit [@tilekbay2024expressedit]; selection-then-instruction as an interaction, as in DirectGPT [@masson2024directgpt]; a converged control vocabulary that supplies the annotation *types* [@vonrutte2023figaro; @guo2022musiac; @chen2024sympac]; and a demonstration that a music agent can act on musician-readable structural feedback in a text grammar, as in Libretto [@xu2026libretto]. The research questions are which annotation types composers actually reach for, how ambiguous marks should be resolved and disambiguated, and how conflicts between annotations are reported rather than silently resolved.

**G2. A lossless bridge between an LLM-friendly textual IR and engraving-grade notation.** *(Nodes S3.1, B1. Dimension D7.)* No verified system in the corpus generates MusicXML or MEI natively. Generation lives in MIDI event tokens, ABC text or piano-roll tensors, and MetaScore converts scores *out* of MuseScore format into REMI+ rather than in [@xu2025metascore]. ABC is the pragmatic candidate because the strongest symbolic music LLMs already speak it, including MuPT's synchronized multi-track SMT-ABC and NotaGen [@qu2024mupt; @wang2025notagen], but ABC cannot express what an engraver needs. Verovio's MEI-native pipeline is the natural target on the notation side [@pugin2014verovio]. Building and evaluating a round-trip that loses nothing a composer cares about is both a prerequisite for the studio and a publishable contribution.

**G3. Notation-editor-native generative assistance.** *(Node S3.2 crossed with dimension D8.)* The DAW side is well served — Composer's Assistant in REAPER, MIDI-GPT in Cubase and Ableton, Magenta Studio, MMM4Live [@malandro2024composersassistant2; @pasquier2025midigpt; @roberts2019magentastudio; @metacreation2023mmm4live]. The notation side has DeepBach's 2017 MuseScore plugin and Hookpad Aria's lead-sheet copilot and essentially nothing else [@hadjeres2017deepbach; @donahue2024hookpadaria]. Dorico, Sibelius and MuseScore Studio ship no generative features as of 2026 [@steinberg2025dorico6; @avid2026sibelius; @musegroup2025musescore46]. Practitioners consistently say tool-switching kills adoption [@deruty2022sonycsl; @newman2023perceptions], and MuseScore is GPL-3 with a DAW-converging roadmap. A notation-native studio has no direct competitor.

**G4. Multimodal editing on a score.** *(Nodes S4.1, S1.3. Dimension D4.)* Sketch-conditioned music generation exists in isolation — contour and density curves in Draw and Listen!, independent pitch and rhythm sketches in Music SketchNet, drawn curves with visual alignment explanations in Drawlody [@benetatos2022drawlisten; @chen2020sketchnet; @liang2024drawlody] — and natural-language plus sketch editing exists for video [@tilekbay2024expressedit]. Nothing combines language, sketch, hum and attached example on notation. Drawlody's usability scores against chord-grid and DAW baselines suggest the drawn contour is worth treating as a first-class annotation type.

**G5. A return channel: the compiler's build log.** *(Node S4.4: two primary entries. Dimension D6.)* COFI's coding of 92 co-creative systems found the AI-to-human direction almost entirely absent [@rezwana2023cofi]. The exceptions are instructive and few: RefinPaint's critic marks *where* a score should be modified before proposing how [@ramoneda2024refinpaint], Cococo offered explanation and debugging affordances [@louie2020cococo], ReaLJam renders committed chords opaque and tentative ones translucent so the human can see the agent's plan before it commits [@scarlatos2025realjam], and ExpressEdit's examine panel shows how each reference was interpreted [@tilekbay2024expressedit]. The compiler metaphor supplies the missing design directly: a build log that states which annotation produced which material, which constraints bound, which were relaxed and why, and where the model was least confident. Work on explaining latent spaces for the arts is the nearest theoretical anchor [@bryankinns2021xai].

**G6. Versioning with musical semantics.** *(Node S4.5: five primary entries.)* Score diffing exists as tree edit distance rendered side by side in Verovio [@foscarin2019diff], Flat.io keeps per-edit history with contributor colouring [@flat2026history], and mei-friend commits MEI to GitHub [@goebl2023meifriend]. Branch and merge with musical semantics does not exist, and variation trees over compiled outputs — the pattern Luminate demonstrates for LLM design-space exploration [@suh2024luminate], with antecedents in Side Views and Chronicle [@terry2002sideviews; @grossman2010chronicle] — are unexplored for music. There is a second motive beyond convenience: a provenance log of human authorship and modification is exactly the record that the authorship analysis in §12.2 makes valuable, a point Micchi and colleagues raised from practice [@micchi2021ikeepcounting].

**G7. Symbolically-conditioned, composer-controllable audio rendering.** *(Node S3.8. Dimension D1.)* Rendering a compiled score to audio under human control is thin and research-grade. JASCO accepts chords, melody salience and drums but ships non-commercial weights; Coco-Mulla, Music ControlNet and MuseControlLite accept chords, MIDI or time-varying curves; MIDI-DDSP renders full MIDI expressively but monophonically; Magenta RealTime 2 accepts live MIDI notes at roughly 200 ms control latency on a laptop [@tal2024jasco; @lin2023cocomulla; @wu2024musiccontrolnet; @tsai2025musecontrollite; @wu2022mididdsp; @lyria2025livemusic]. No commercial full-song system accepts MIDI or chords as a *generation* condition. Inference-time optimization is the most promising general mechanism, since it turns any differentiable musical feature into a constraint on a frozen model without retraining [@novack2024ditto].

**G8. Interactive correction for loose input.** *(Node S1.2.)* On a clean humming corpus with corrected onset labels, off-the-shelf transcribers reach 0.43–0.60 note-plus-onset F1 and a purpose-built CNN-plus-dynamic-programming model roughly 0.67 [@liu2023humtrans; @gupta2024dynhumtrans]. Humming will therefore always need disambiguation — key and scale locking, quantisation grids, smoothing, click-to-fix — rather than a claim of correctness [@doremir2014scorecloud; @vochlea2021dubler]. The transferable principle comes from an unexpected place: one targeted human annotation, clicking repeat signs, lifted audio-to-score alignment from 33% to 82% [@bukey2024justlabel]. Cheap, well-placed human correction beats full automation, and designing where to spend the composer's attention is a research question in its own right.

**G9. Editable structural skeletons.** *(Node S3.4.)* Dai and Dannenberg's analyses established that deep generative outputs differ strikingly from human popular music in repetition and hierarchical structure [@dai2022missing; @dai2024interconnections]. The answers already point at the annotation layer: MusicFrameworks makes section and phrase structure, chords and a basic melody explicit user-editable inputs, and the whole-song cascaded diffusion model exposes four editable levels from form down to accompaniment [@dai2021controllable; @wang2024wholesong]. A studio whose compile step reads a human-authored form annotation inherits both the structural fix and the control surface.

**G10. Evaluating the loop rather than the artifact.** *(Nodes E1, E4.)* Distributional audio metrics correlate weakly with human preference — Fréchet Audio Distance has been argued to be superseded, and benchmarks against 15,000 human pairwise preferences show weak metric-human agreement [@chung2025kad; @grotschla2025benchmarking; @huang2025aligning]. The instruments that matter for a co-creative tool exist — the Creativity Support Index, ownership and control items, paired listener comparisons, and Karimi and colleagues' warning that co-creative systems over-measure user experience and under-measure the creativity of the collaboration [@cherry2014csi; @karimi2018evaluating] — but longitudinal, in-practice studies remain rare [@krol2025ownership; @deruty2022sonycsl]. Suggestion-level acceptance telemetry of the kind Hookpad Aria reports is the most under-used measure available [@donahue2024hookpadaria].

**G11. Orchestration without over-trusting the orchestrator.** *(Node E6.)* Specialised symbolic music LLMs outperform general models on composition tasks [@wang2025notagen; @qu2024mupt], but every benchmark that probes musical reasoning reports weakness: ZIQI-Eval found all sixteen models tested poor, MuChoMusic showed audio-language models over-relying on text, and probing work questions whether generation models encode theory concepts at all [@li2024ziqieval; @weck2024muchomusic; @wei2024musictheory]. The architecture this implies is an LLM as orchestrator around specialised symbolic models and deterministic analysers — with the analysers, not the language model, holding authority over theory.

**G12. Licence-clean symbolic training data at scale.** *(Node B4. Dimension D10.)* The clean sets are real but small relative to what the field trains on: PDMX at more than 250,000 public-domain MusicXML scores, OpenScore's CC0 editions, GiantMIDI-Piano, ATEPP and the Bach Doodle corpus [@long2024pdmx; @gotham2022openscore; @kong2020giantmidi; @zhang2022atepp; @huang2019bachdoodle]. The large and convenient sets are not: Aria-MIDI's 1.19 million files are CC-BY-NC-SA, Hooktheory's data is non-commercial, and scraped MuseScore dumps are legally untenable [@bradshaw2025ariamidi; @xmader2021musescore]. Composer's Assistant's decision to train only on permissively licensed MIDI shows the constraint is workable [@malandro2024composersassistant2]. Assembling and documenting a shippable symbolic training corpus, with an EU AI Act-style training-content summary, is unglamorous infrastructure work with outsized value to an open project.

### 13.2 A sequenced agenda

The gaps sort naturally into three horizons.

The **near horizon** is engineering that unblocks everything else and is publishable as systems work: the notation round-trip of G2, an annotation data model that reuses MEI or Dezrann's record shape rather than inventing one (G1's substrate), integration with an existing notation engine rather than a new renderer (G3), and the licence-clean corpus of G12. None of this requires new modelling. Existing infilling models can be wrapped immediately — the Anticipatory Music Transformer's anticipation tokens map directly onto "everything the composer wrote is a hard constraint," and it runs in a browser [@thickstun2024anticipatory; @zhou2024midinfinite].

The **middle horizon** is the studio's actual research contribution: the annotation compiler of G1, evaluated against the instruments of G10, with the build log of G5 as its distinguishing feature. This is where a defensible claim lies, because it is simultaneously an interaction contribution, a systems contribution and an evaluation contribution, and because the corpus contains no competitor. The multimodal editing surface of G4 and the structural skeleton of G9 are natural components of the same system rather than separate projects.

The **far horizon** is rendering and performance: symbolically-conditioned audio under composer control (G7), and the real-time branch, where the RL-tuned accompaniment line from ReaLchords through ReaLJam to post-training fixes for reward hacking has produced open code and demonstrated interaction patterns worth building on [@wu2024realchords; @scarlatos2025realjam; @wu2026gapt].

### 13.3 Concluding argument

Three claims stand up across the whole corpus, and together they make the case for the studio.

The first is that the composer-centered loop is not a contrarian preference but the direction every serious line of evidence points. Coconet described nonlinear scribbling and revision as the thing to model in 2017 [@huang2017coconet]. The controlled studies found that steering interfaces move composer control, ownership and self-efficacy even when the model is held fixed, and that interface and model gains are complementary [@louie2020cococo; @louie2022expressive]. The field studies found practitioners decomposing songs into parts, generating into those parts and curating, and asking for systems that are decomposable, steerable and interpretable [@huang2020aisongcontest; @morris2024haisp]. The one deployment with public telemetry shows musicians accepting span-level suggestions inside a notation editor at scale [@donahue2024hookpadaria]. And the commercial systems built on the opposite premise keep adding editing affordances after the fact [@suno2026; @google2025lyria2].

The second is that the technical substrate is unusually ready. Infilling under hard constraints is mature and, in several cases, permissively licensed and small enough to run locally [@thickstun2024anticipatory; @pasquier2025midigpt; @malandro2024composersassistant2]. Notation rendering, symbolic parsing and analysis are solved well enough to adopt rather than rebuild [@pugin2014verovio; @cuthbert2010music21; @liao2024symusic; @fradet2021miditok]. Hierarchical pipelines with editable intermediate levels already exist as architectural templates [@wang2024wholesong; @dai2021controllable]. Decompiling symbolic music into an editable program has been demonstrated with execution-based rewards, which makes "music as code" an empirical result rather than a metaphor [@kim2026decomposer].

The third is that the gap between what exists and what a composer needs is concentrated almost entirely in the *annotation and feedback* layer — the two thinnest load-bearing nodes in the taxonomy, S2.4 and S4.4. That is a fortunate place for a gap to be. It is an interaction-design and systems problem more than a frontier-modelling problem, which means a small open-source project with a clear thesis can make real progress on it without competing for compute. It is also the layer that determines whether the human stays at the center, since annotations are how intent enters and the build log is how the machine accounts for what it did.

The studio's premise, restated in the vocabulary this review has assembled: a score-driven, transformative system in the instrument paradigm [@rowe1993interactive], with alternating turn-taking [@kantosalo2016modes], bidirectional communication [@rezwana2023cofi], multi-level editable intermediate representations, and the AI in the role of colleague at compile time and coach when it annotates back [@lubart2005partners] — never author.
