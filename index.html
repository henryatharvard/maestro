Maestro

The composer stays at the centre. The machine compiles.

Most music AI now takes a sentence and returns a finished master. That is the wrong shape for someone who actually writes music. Maestro studies the opposite arrangement — the composer authors and marks up the material, and the model compiles a fuller realization under those marks, over and over, at the granularity of a bar or a part. This site publishes the research openly as it develops. It begins with a survey of where the field actually stands.

Henry Tan · Harvard University · github.com/henryatharvard/maestro

§ 1

Why not text-to-song

A prompt is a narrow channel for musical intent, and the output arrives as an opaque mixdown with nothing to grab hold of. That is not a taste objection — it is what professionals report when they are handed these tools, and it is what the market keeps conceding by bolting editing features onto architectures that were never designed to iterate.

Infilling, not generation, is the primitive

Filling a masked region while everything the composer wrote stays a hard constraint. Nine years of work has made this mature, and the one deployment with public telemetry shows musicians accepting span-level suggestions inside a notation editor at scale.

318,000 suggestions · 74,000 accepted · ~23% · Hookpad Aria

The interface moves control as much as the model does

In the two controlled studies that varied model and interface independently, steering tools raised a composer's sense of control and ownership even with the model held fixed. Better models and better interfaces turn out to be complementary, not substitutes.

controllability 5.9 vs 3.5 / 7 · N=21 · Cococo, CHI 2020

Every input that works lands in notation

Humming becomes MIDI, a drawn contour becomes a melody, an image becomes chords. The symbolic layer is what gives a human something to correct. Systems that map a loose gesture straight to audio are impressive and uncorrectable.

S1.1–S1.5 · 47 works surveyed

The annotation layer is empty

Score annotation has standards. Analysis models can propose labels. Generators have converged on a control vocabulary that reads like a list of things a composer would write on a page. Nobody has connected them. This is the opening.

§ 4

Open directions

Twelve gaps were derived by intersecting the thin nodes of the taxonomy with what a composer-centered system actually requires. Each names the nearest prior art, so none of them starts from nothing. They are published here because they are worth more to the field than to any one person — take them.

G1

Annotations as a compilable instruction set

S2.4

Every part exists and none are assembled. MEI and Dezrann give typed, musical-time-anchored annotation records. EuterPen lets composers put ink, images and audio on and between staves. ExpressEdit shows how to parse “language plus a sketch on the artifact” into typed references with per-modality resolvers — for video. Nobody has built the music equivalent.

Nearest prior art: ExpressEdit (IUI 2024) · Dezrann · MEI · DirectGPT · Libretto
G2

A lossless bridge between a text IR and engraved notation

S3.1 · B1

No verified system generates MusicXML or MEI natively. Generation lives in MIDI tokens, ABC or piano-roll tensors; MetaScore converts scores out rather than in. A round-trip that loses nothing a composer cares about is both the first engineering task and an open problem.

Nearest prior art: MuPT's SMT-ABC · NotaGen · Verovio · MusicXML 4.0
G5

A return channel: the compiler's build log

S4.4

A survey of 92 co-creative systems found machine-to-human communication almost entirely absent. The compiler framing answers this directly: state which annotation produced which material, which constraints bound, which were relaxed, and where the model was least sure.

Nearest prior art: RefinPaint's critic · ReaLJam's committed-vs-tentative display · Cococo
G6

Versioning with musical semantics

S4.5

Score diffing exists and notation editors keep edit history, but branch and merge that understand music do not. There is a second motive beyond convenience: a provenance log of human authorship is exactly the record that current copyright doctrine makes valuable.

Nearest prior art: Foscarin et al.'s tree-edit-distance diff · Luminate · mei-friend
G7

Composer-controllable audio rendering

S3.8

Rendering a compiled score to audio under human control is thin and research-grade. No commercial full-song system accepts MIDI or chords as a generation condition. Inference-time optimization looks like the general mechanism: it turns any differentiable musical feature into a constraint on a frozen model without retraining.

Nearest prior art: JASCO · Music ControlNet · MuseControlLite · DITTO · MIDI-DDSP
G10

Evaluating the loop, not the artefact

E1 · E4

Distributional audio metrics correlate weakly with human preference. The instruments that suit a co-creative tool already exist — creativity support indices, ownership and control items, paired listener tests, suggestion-acceptance telemetry — but longitudinal studies in real practice remain rare.

Nearest prior art: Creativity Support Index · Karimi et al. · Hookpad Aria telemetry
The remaining six directions
  1. G3 — Notation-editor-native assistance. The DAW side is well served; the notation side has two deployments in the entire corpus, and Dorico, Sibelius and MuseScore ship nothing generative.
  2. G4 — Multimodal editing on a score. Sketch-conditioned generation exists in isolation; language plus sketch plus hum plus example, applied to notation, does not.
  3. G8 — Interactive correction for loose input. Humming transcription leaves roughly one note in three wrong at the state of the art, so disambiguation is a design requirement, not a nicety.
  4. G9 — Editable structural skeletons. Deep generators still differ strikingly from human music in repetition and hierarchy; explicit, human-editable form is the remedy.
  5. G11 — Orchestration without over-trusting the orchestrator. Every benchmark probing musical reasoning in language models reports weakness. Use them as glue around deterministic analysers, not as the analyser.
  6. G12 — Licence-clean symbolic training data at scale. The clean corpora are real but small; the convenient ones are not shippable. Assembling and documenting one is unglamorous work with outsized value to an open project.
§ 5

Use it, argue with it

What this is for

The survey is meant to save other people the six months it took to assemble. Cite it, fork it, lift the taxonomy, disagree with the gap list in public. Corrections are the most valuable contribution — the repository has an issue template for one, and every claim in the review is traceable to a source note so a correction can be checked.

What comes next

The near work is unglamorous and blocks everything else: the notation round-trip (G2), an annotation data model that reuses an existing standard rather than inventing one, and a licence-clean corpus. After that, the annotation compiler itself — with the build log as the thing that distinguishes it.
